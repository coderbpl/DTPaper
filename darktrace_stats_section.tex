\section{Statistical Validation}\label{sec:stats}
All comparisons report central tendency with dispersion and significance, using
McNemar's test for paired classifier predictions, paired bootstrap for metric
differences, exact Wilson score intervals for per-language accuracy, and a
Friedman$+$Nemenyi omnibus test across $\geq 3$ models, with Holm--Bonferroni
correction over the resulting $p$-values ($\alpha=0.05$). Tables~\ref{tab:stats-cls}
and~\ref{tab:stats-ml} report measured values from the executed experiments
(seed~$=42$); cells marked ``re-run'' require the prediction artifacts now emitted by
the patched experiment scripts.

\subsection{Classification and Severity Scoring}
On the CoDA text corpus, both TF-IDF baselines are tightly estimated and the
Linear-SVM advantage over Logistic Regression is statistically significant
(McNemar $\chi^2=12.44$, $p=4.19\times10^{-4}$; Table~\ref{tab:stats-cls}). For severity
scoring, the explainable ensemble differs significantly from the black-box MLP
(McNemar $p=2.79\times10^{-4}$) while its $5$-fold and bootstrap intervals overlap the
baseline's ranking metrics---i.e.\ \framework{} adds interpretability and better
calibration without a statistically meaningful loss in discrimination.

\subsection{Multilingual Detection: Per-Language Wilson Intervals}
Table~\ref{tab:stats-ml} reports exact Wilson $95\%$ intervals for per-language accuracy.
These make the small-sample caveat explicit and unavoidable: the apparently perfect
Portuguese result (acc $=1.000$) rests on only $N=10$ items and carries a Wilson interval
of $[0.723,\,1.000]$, and German/Spanish/French pooled cells ($N=24/15/25$) are similarly
wide. Only the well-sampled languages (EN $N\!\approx\!1.7$k; RU $N=114$/$542$) support
firm claims. We therefore restrict headline multilingual conclusions to EN and RU and
treat the remaining languages as exploratory pending more data.

\subsection{Reproducing and Completing the Battery}
The statistical layer is implemented in \texttt{src/metrics\_stats.py} (McNemar, paired
bootstrap, Wilson CI, Friedman$+$Nemenyi, Holm--Bonferroni) and driven by
\texttt{src/exp\_stats.py}, which writes \texttt{stats\_results.json} and
\texttt{table\_stats.csv}. The experiment scripts now persist per-instance predictions
to \texttt{darktrace\_results/preds/}; re-running \texttt{python -m src.exp\_stats} after
a real experiment run additionally emits (i)~the paired-bootstrap macro-F1 difference for
explainable-vs-black-box scoring, (ii)~per-language macro-F1 bootstrap CIs, and (iii)~the
Friedman$+$Nemenyi critical-difference diagram across classifiers.
